Section 1 · Case summaryEyes only

What we know

The government paid psychics to spy. This is not the theory part.

Starting in 1972, the CIA funded research at the Stanford Research Institute into whether a person could see distant places using only their mind. The work moved to the U.S. Army and the Defense Intelligence Agency and ran out of old wooden buildings at Fort Meade, Maryland.

The skill was called remote viewing. A viewer gets a random number that stands for a hidden target (a building, a person, a submarine) and describes or sketches it.

Why? A 1972 DIA report warned that the Soviet Union was researching psychic warfare. If they had psychics, we needed psychics.

In 1995 the program was reviewed, called useless, shut down and declassified. Allegedly.

Section 2 · PersonnelSubjects of interest

The operatives

Artists, cops, physicists and one spoon bender.

Viewer · The star

Ingo Swann

New York artist who helped invent “coordinate remote viewing,” describing a target from nothing but a number. Said Jupiter had a ring in 1973.

Viewer · Remote Viewer #001

Joseph McMoneagle

Army intelligence officer. Described a giant Soviet submarine in 1979 that nobody in the U.S. knew existed.

Viewer · Ex-cop

Pat Price

Former police commissioner from Burbank, California. Sketched a crane at a secret Soviet site in 1974. Died suddenly in 1975.

Founders · Physicists

Russell Targ & Hal Puthoff

Laser physicists at the Stanford Research Institute who started the research in 1972 with CIA money.

Guest · Spoon bender

Uri Geller

Yes, that one. Tested at the Stanford Research Institute in 1973.

Reviewer · Statistician

Jessica Utts

Hired for the 1995 review. Concluded psychic functioning was “well established.” The program was cancelled anyway.

Section 3 · Evidence locker6 exhibits

The evidence

Six things that should not have worked.

A

April 1973

A psychic “visited” Jupiter and saw a ring.

Ingo Swann was given one target: the planet Jupiter. He described a ring around it. As far as science knew, Jupiter had no rings. In 1979, NASA’s Voyager 1 found one.

They sayLucky guess. Planets have rings.

I sayName one other person who called it in 1973.

Open the file

Pioneer 10 flew past Jupiter months after Swann’s session and didn’t spot a ring. It took Voyager 1, six years later, to confirm a faint ring system.

B

Late 1970s · told in 1995

A President said it out loud.

A Soviet plane went down in the jungle in Zaire and U.S. satellites couldn’t find it. Jimmy Carter later said: “She went into a trance. And while she was in the trance, she gave us some latitude and longitude figures. We focused our satellite cameras on that point, and the plane was there.”

They sayHe was telling a fun story to college students.

I sayJimmy Carter does not do bits.

Open the file

Carter told the story to students in 1995, the same year the program was declassified. Nobel Peace Prize winner. Sunday school teacher. Not known for making things up.

C

1979

He found a submarine nobody knew existed.

Joseph McMoneagle was shown a photo of a huge building at a Soviet shipyard. He described a giant new submarine inside and said it would launch in about four months. Satellites later caught the Typhoon-class sub, still the largest submarine ever built.

They sayHe guessed from the size of the building.

I sayThen why didn’t the professional analysts?

Open the file

McMoneagle was officially Remote Viewer #001. He has written several books about his time in the program.

D

May 22, 1984

The CIA sent a psychic to Mars. One million years ago.

A declassified CIA document titled “Mars Exploration.” A viewer opens a sealed envelope: the planet Mars, about one million years B.C. They describe pyramids, very tall people, and a dying civilization looking for a new home.

They sayIt was one strange session.

I sayThen why was it classified?

Open the file

This one is real and public. Search “Mars Exploration” in the CIA Reading Room (link in Sources) and read it yourself. Ideally not alone.

Bonus file
E

1983

The Army’s guide to leaving your body.

An Army report by Lt. Col. Wayne McDonnell analyzed the Monroe Institute’s “Gateway Experience”: headphone tones meant to sync both halves of your brain and let your consciousness slip outside time and space. Declassified in 2003. For years, page 25 was missing from the public copy.

They sayA clerical error.

I sayLosing one page of the leave-your-body manual?

Bonus file
F

1974

The ex-cop who drew a secret crane.

Pat Price, a former Burbank police commissioner, was given coordinates for a secret Soviet site. He sketched a giant gantry crane. Later intelligence showed a crane there that looked a lot like his drawing.

They saySome details matched. Lots didn’t.

I sayHe died suddenly the next year. Just saying.

About $20M over 23 years. Black budgets don’t just stop.
